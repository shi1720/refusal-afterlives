Topic selection and contribution boundary
Broad proposals about AI companions, consent in romance, paid romantic choices,
and rejected marriage proposals have extensive precedents. They were not treated
as undiscovered topics. The selected contribution is an executable, source-specific
analysis of opposite motives with identical branch bodies, asymmetric acceptance
memory, and the distinction between romantic closure and serial eligibility.

The literature search was targeted and iterative, not a systematic review. Searches
included consent mechanics, romance-game agency, premium choices, Choice of Romance
criticism, refusal in that work, interactive narrative verification, and romance
scholarship on consent. No search establishes the absence of all prior overlapping
work. In particular, Ma and Short already discuss uneven story lengths and court
intrigue in this game; Nguyen and Ruberg already introduce consent mechanics;
Moreno-Ger and colleagues already establish game model checking. The manuscript
explicitly acknowledges these precedents and does not claim those ideas as new.

Commercial context
Kim's study of Choices and Episode documents an established commercial setting
for monetized interactive fiction. The selected publisher's own Episode2 source
shows a sequel purchase boundary. Those observations motivate an application to
narrative QA and localization. They do not establish market size, customer demand
for this particular tool, revenue gains, or patentability. No such claim is made.

Evidence status

intervention pairs, and 17,372 bounded local executions were actually run. The
independent model agreed on all checked local destinations and values. The

diagnostic histories. Full-episode random traversal is not exhaustive verification.
